# Part 3 — Add-ons & Extensions

## Add-on Feature List

### 1. Automated Compliance Checklist Generator

When a regulatory change is matched to a customer, the system auto-generates an actionable checklist of concrete steps the customer needs to take (e.g., "update permit application for Anlage 3.1.1", "revise internal operating procedures for CMR 1B handling", "notify competent authority within 3 months"). Each checklist item links back to the specific legal provision and the customer's relevant properties, so consultants can validate and customers can execute.

### 2. Customer Self-Service Dashboard

A web portal where customers can log in, see which regulatory changes affect them, read plain-language summaries, download compliance checklists, and confirm they've completed required actions. This reduces consultant workload for routine notifications and gives customers a tangible reason to pay a premium for the monitoring service.

### 3. Natural Language Change Summaries (LLM-Powered)

An LLM pipeline that reads the diff between old and new regulation text and produces a one-paragraph, customer-specific summary in plain German (e.g., "Your Anlage 3.1.1 at 8 t/h is now above the new 5 t/h threshold. You must apply for a full operating permit within 6 months."). The summary is always generated alongside the structured match data and presented in the consultant review UI and the customer dashboard.

### 4. Cross-Regulation Cascade Analysis

When a regulation changes, the system doesn't just match that single regulation — it also checks whether the change triggers obligations under *other* regulations. For example, a threshold change in BImSchG §5 may also affect KrWG waste reporting duties or GefStoffV substance handling requirements for the same facility. This produces a dependency graph of related changes that the consultant can review as a cluster rather than in isolation.

### 5. Regulatory Change Forecast & Transition Calendar

A quarterly view showing not only what changed, but what is *expected* to change based on pending legislation, EU directive transposition deadlines, and known amendment cycles. Customers and consultants see a timeline with color-coded urgency, transition periods, and deadlines — turning reactive compliance into proactive planning.

## Deep Dive: Automated Compliance Checklist Generator

### What Exactly Is Being Built?

A component that sits between the relevance matching pipeline (Part 2) and the notification layer. For each confirmed `ChangeCustomerMatch` with `review_status == APPROVED`, it produces a structured checklist: an ordered list of actionable items, each with a deadline estimate, the legal basis, the customer property that triggered it, and a completion status field.

The checklist is not a free-text blob. It is generated by matching the *type of change* and the *customer's profile facts* against a library of templated action patterns. For example:

```
Pattern: THRESHOLD_TIGHTENING + customer has facility above new threshold
→ Checklist items:
  1. "Apply for full operating permit (Vollgenehmigung)" — deadline: 6 months
  2. "Update internal environmental management documentation" — deadline: 3 months
  3. "Notify competent authority of changed obligations" — deadline: 1 month
```

### Why Is It Valuable?

**For consultants:** The current workflow ends at "this customer is affected." The hard part begins *after* that — figuring out what the customer should actually do. The checklist generator encodes domain knowledge about typical compliance obligations for each change type, so consultants spend less time researching and more time reviewing edge cases. It also standardizes the quality of advice across consultants and customers.

**For customers:** Instead of receiving a vague notification ("BImSchG §5 changed, this may affect you"), they receive a concrete to-do list with deadlines. This is the difference between a monitoring service and a compliance service — and it's a clear upsell path.

### High-Level Implementation Sketch

**Components:**

1. **ActionPattern library** — a declarative data store (JSON/SQL) of rules that map `(change_type, customer_property_combination)` → `[checklist_items]`. Each pattern has:
   - A matcher (same condition syntax as `ApplicabilityRule.conditions`)
   - One or more checklist item templates (title, description, deadline_offset, legal_basis_reference, priority)
   - A `confidence` field so consultants know which items are deterministic vs. advisory

2. **ChecklistGenerator service** — runs after consultant approval. Takes the approved `ChangeCustomerMatch`, the customer profile, and the change record. Evaluates all matching ActionPatterns, instantiates templates with customer-specific values, and deduplicates.

3. **Checklist persistence** — stored as `CustomerChecklist` records with per-item completion tracking. Tied to the customer's notification history for audit.

**Data flow:**

```
Change detected → Relevance match → Consultant approves → ChecklistGenerator
  → evaluates ActionPatterns against customer profile
  → produces CustomerChecklist
  → persists to DB
  → triggers notification (email + dashboard update)
```

**Key decisions:**
- Patterns are maintained by consultants, not hardcoded. This keeps the system adaptable to new regulation types without software releases.
- Deadline estimates are conservative (e.g., "6 months" is a safe default; the pattern library can be tuned per regulation).
- The generator runs *after* consultant approval, not before. This avoids presenting auto-generated advice that the consultant hasn't validated the match for.

### Biggest Risk / Challenge

**Accuracy of generated checklists.** If the system tells a customer "you must apply for a permit within 6 months" and the actual deadline is 3 months, the customer is exposed to regulatory risk. Conversely, if it invents obligations that don't exist, the system loses credibility.

Mitigation strategy:
- Every checklist item carries a `confidence` level and a `source_pattern_id`. Low-confidence items are flagged as "advisory — verify with consultant."
- The consultant review UI includes a checklist preview step. The consultant can edit, remove, or add items before approval.
- A feedback loop tracks which checklist items customers mark as "not applicable" or which lead to follow-up questions. Patterns with high correction rates are flagged for review.
- Legal basis references are always included, so both consultant and customer can independently verify the obligation.
